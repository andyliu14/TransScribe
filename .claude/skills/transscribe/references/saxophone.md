# Saxophones

Specify the family member before laying out the part. Written C5 sounds E♭4 on alto, B♭3 on tenor, B♭4 on soprano and E♭3 on baritone. Use treble clef. In this harness the alto’s preferred *sounding* G3–D5 is a register preference for sustained chamber writing. Check the exported part by playing back a written C against the expected concert note.

Treat a slur as an articulation instruction. A slurred ascent can grow on one airstream, while repeated notes usually need fresh tongue attacks. In a classical relay, let cello take the cadence while the saxophonist inhales. In a swing chart, specify feel and articulate accents that shape the offbeats. Scoops, subtone, growls and altissimo need a stylistic reason and a player comfortable with the technique.

Try singing the line at tempo through all repeats. A two-bar rest on paper can disappear on a first-ending jump. Inspect quiet attacks at the bottom, palm-key peaks and wide leaps across registers. Test whether the player can re-enter softly after breathing. Mark a breath at a musically usable release, preserving the pulse in another part.
